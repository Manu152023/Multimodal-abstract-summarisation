      Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition

      Kaiming He, Xiangyu Zhang, Shaoqing Ren, Jian Sun
      Microsoft Research, Redmond, WA, USA
      Abstract—Deeper neural networks are more difficult to train. We present a residual learning framework to ease the training of networks that are substantially deeper than those previously used. We explicitly reformulate the layers as learning residual functions with reference to the layer inputs, instead of learning unreferenced functions.

      1. Research Motivation & Background

      In recent years, deep convolutional neural networks have led to a series of breakthroughs for image classification. Deep networks naturally integrate low/mid/high-level features and classifiers in an end-to-end multi-layer fashion, and the "levels" of features can be enriched by the number of stacked layers (depth).

      However, network depth is of crucial importance, and a key problem is: Is learning better networks as easy as stacking more layers? When deeper networks are able to start converging, a degradation problem has been exposed: with the network depth increasing, accuracy gets saturated and then degrades rapidly. Stacking more layers leads to higher training error.

      2. Proposed Methodology

      Instead of hoping each few stacked layers directly fit a desired underlying mapping $\mathcal{H}(x)$, we explicitly let these layers fit a residual mapping $\mathcal{F}(x) := \mathcal{H}(x) - x$. The original mapping is reformulated into $\mathcal{F}(x) + x$. We hypothesize that it is easier to optimize the residual mapping than to optimize the original, unreferenced mapping.

      Figure 1: Deep residual learning: a building block.

      3. Addressed Research Gap

      Traditional convolutional networks suffer from extreme performance degradation due to vanishing gradients during backpropagation. Identity shortcut mappings directly bypass parameters, preserving feature coordinates across depth stacks without parameter additions.

      4. Experimental Ablation Studies

      We perform comprehensive ablation runs on ImageNet datasets to examine the influence of skip shortcut connections. We compare plain networks (stacking layers without shortcuts) against deep residual networks of identical parameters.

      Architecture Top-1 Error (%) Top-5 Error (%) Params (M)
      Plain-34 28.54 10.12 21.6
      ResNet-34 (Ours) 25.03 7.76 21.6
      ResNet-152 (Ours) 21.43 5.71 60.2

      Our ResNet models converge much faster and achieve significantly lower error, verifying that identity shortcuts resolve degradation issues at scale.

      3. E. Hu, Y. Shen, P. Wallis, et al. "LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models." International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR), 2022.
